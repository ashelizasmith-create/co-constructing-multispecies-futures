Co-Constructing Multispecies Futures

About & cite

A Card Deck and Interactive Tool for embodied worldbuilding through performance and play.

Build and tell speculative scenes, then enter them through roleplay, movement, sensing, and rehearsal. What happens returns to reflection, writing, and another encounter. The tools support this shared practice with people, animals, plants, materials, machines, and places.

The framework: imagine, rehearse, reflect.

A group may begin by talking, drawing, or imagining at a table, then enter the scene through a role, a movement, or a relation. What happens in rehearsal returns to discussion, sketches, and writing. Those changes then shape the next embodied encounter. This is an iterative practice, with the body's senses and constraints contributing to what can be known and made.

EncounteringMeet another being or force in a scene. It might be voiced by a person, embodied by a machine, or approached through an object or situation.

Moving withMove with a body, system, city, or landscape. Follow its rhythms and notice where its rules and constraints shape what you can do.

Becoming-withLet a relation change how you sense and move. Taking up another position, scale, or perceptual constraint is one way to begin.

These approaches overlap. Becoming-with follows Haraway's relational account (2008, 2016): the participant remains themselves while the relation changes what they notice. Another being's perspective can only be approached partially. Attend to its capacities, needs, and limits, including what it resists or cannot do.

Adapt participation through attention, orientation, gesture, scale, and constraint to suit the bodies present. Agree how authorship and creative decisions will be shared. Make room to mark the end, reflect together, and choose what to carry forward or set down. A rehearsal can become a story, artwork, prototype, or technology; it can also remain a gesture, question, or unresolved experience.

The deck's lineage.

The chapter explicitly identifies two foundations for the Co-Constructing Multispecies Futures Card Deck and Interactive Tool:

  • Candy, S., & Watson, J. (2014). The thing from the future [Card game]. Situation Lab.
  • Smith, A. E., Leadabrand, M., Lichtman, D., Bendix, S., PytlikZillig, L. M., & McQuillan, J. (in press). Rehearsing realities: A dramaturgy for co-creating preferable futures. TDR/The Drama Review.

A wider performance and design practice

The chapter locates the wider practice in performance, dance and somatic practice, walking scores, speculative design, spatial storytelling, playable theater, and immersive practice. It connects with thing ethnography, multispecies imagining, empathic modelling, and embodiment exchanges. These neighboring practices inform ways of approaching more-than-human worlds without claiming to fully inhabit another's experience.
